Dear Editor,

Please consider the manuscript "Priority boarding in lift group control: a simulation study of its cost to other passengers and of a priority-aware destination control rule" for publication in Simulation Modelling Practice and Theory.

The paper contributes to simulation methodology and its application to vertical transportation. It presents an open, seconds-based discrete-event lift simulator, verified by automated tests and checked against the classical up-peak round-trip-time calculation (agreement within 2%), and a paired experimental design (31,680 runs with common random numbers) in which every measured passenger is followed for a fixed minimum period, so that a capped mean waiting time over all arrivals is observed exactly. It reports (1) the cost and benefit of priority-first boarding for passengers with access requirements under five group controllers, steady and time-varying demand and two passenger profiles, with paired-bootstrap intervals; (2) the finding that priority at the door has no measurable effect under destination control until it saturates, together with a fully specified priority-aware allocation rule, its benefit in normal operation and its cost under sustained overload; and (3) a check that the conventional served-passenger average ranks controllers like the capped mean.

The limitations are stated plainly: the model is validated analytically, not against measured building data, and the controllers are transparent implementations of published principles. The test plan, simulator, tests, run-level results and analysis programs are public at https://github.com/alanvallavaraj/liftsim, and the model is reported following the STRESS-DES guidelines.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and has a single author with no competing interests. Generative AI tools were used as declared in the manuscript.
