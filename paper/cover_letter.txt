Dear Editor-in-Chief,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "Priority boarding in lift group control: a simulation study of its cost to other passengers and of a priority-aware destination control rule", for publication as a research paper in Building Services Engineering Research and Technology.

Passengers with access requirements, such as wheelchair users, may be given priority when boarding a lift. Lift designers and building operators have, to my knowledge, no published figures for what this costs the other passengers, or for whether the policy works with destination control. The manuscript answers both questions with an open discrete-event simulator of a lift group. The simulator is verified by automated tests and agrees with the classical up-peak round-trip time within 2%. Five group controllers, two boarding rules and two priority-passenger profiles are compared in 31,680 paired runs.

The findings that matter for practice are:

- With landing-call control, priority-first boarding costs other passengers 1.8 s on average at the up-peak handling capacity and saves priority passengers 9.3 s. The cost grows only in heavily loaded periods.
- With destination control, priority at the door has no measurable effect in normal operation. The priority label has to be used when cars are allocated, and the paper specifies and tests a rule that does this.
- Passengers who need more space reduce handling capacity for everyone by more than boarding order changes any wait, so capacity planning should count them in places, not persons.

I believe the paper suits the journal because it builds directly on lift traffic analysis published here (Al-Sharif et al., 2012, on the up-peak round-trip time) and on CIBSE Guide D, and because its conclusions are addressed to lift designers and building operators. The model is reported following the STRESS-DES guidelines, and all code, tests and run-level results are public at https://github.com/alanvallavaraj/liftsim.

I confirm that the manuscript is original, has not been published and is not under consideration by another journal. A preprint of this work was posted on Preprints.org on 18 November 2026 (https://doi.org/10.20944/preprints202611.1795.v1). I am the sole author. The work received no funding, I have no conflicting interests, and no human participants were involved. The use of generative AI tools is declared in the manuscript.

The manuscript has about 7,700 words, 10 figures and 11 tables. A plain-language summary and a glossary are provided as supplementary material.
