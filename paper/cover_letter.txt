Dear Editor,

Please consider the manuscript "Priority boarding in lift groups: its cost to other passengers and a priority-aware destination control rule" for publication in Simulation Modelling Practice and Theory.

The paper contributes to simulation methodology and application in vertical transportation. It presents an open, seconds-based discrete-event lift simulator validated against the classical up-peak round-trip-time calculation (agreement within 2%), and a paired experimental design (31,680 runs with common random numbers) in which every measured passenger is followed for a fixed minimum period, so that an all-arrival restricted mean waiting time is fully observed. It reports: (1) the cost and benefit of priority-first boarding for passengers with accessibility needs, for five group controllers, under steady and time-varying demand, including passengers who need more space and time; (2) the finding that priority at the door has no effect under destination control up to handling capacity, and a new priority-aware allocation rule that reduces priority passengers' waiting by 30-43% in normal operation; and (3) a check that the conventional served-passenger average ranks controllers like the restricted mean. The paper is written in plain language with explanatory diagrams so that practitioners can follow it.

The protocol was fixed before execution, and the simulator, tests, run-level results and analysis scripts are publicly archived. The model is reported following the STRESS-DES guidelines.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and has a single author with no competing interests.

Yours sincerely,
