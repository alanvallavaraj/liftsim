\title{Priority boarding in lift groups: its cost to other passengers and a priority-aware destination control rule}
\author{Alan Immanuel Benjamin Vallavaraj (\texttt{a.vallavaraj@westminster.ac.uk})}
\address{University of Westminster, London, United Kingdom}
\begin{abstract}
Some lift passengers, such as wheelchair users, should board before other people. This study measures what such a priority rule costs the other passengers, and proposes a way to make it work under destination control. We built an open lift simulator that works in seconds, with real car movement, door times and boarding times; its round-trip times agree with the classical formula within 2\%. Five group controllers, two boarding rules and two kinds of priority passenger were compared in 31,680 runs with identical passengers, for three buildings, steady demand from 50\% to 130\% of handling capacity and demand that rises and falls. Every counted passenger was watched for at least 900~s, so the average wait of all passengers, capped at 600~s, is exact. With call buttons, priority-first boarding costs other passengers at most 3.3~s below capacity and 1.8~s on average at capacity, where priority passengers gain 9.3~s; at 115\% of capacity the gain is 81~s and the cost 14~s. Wheelchair-like passengers, who take three places and board slowly, lower capacity for everyone, an effect several times larger than that of the boarding order. Under destination control, priority at the door has no effect up to capacity, because each passenger can board only one car. A new priority-aware rule that uses the priority label when choosing the car cuts the wait of priority passengers by 30\% to 43\% in normal operation, at a cost of 1 to 4~s for others, but is expensive in heavy overload. The usual average over served passengers ranks the controllers like the capped average in 659 of 660 comparisons.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Data and code availability}
The protocol, simulator, automated tests, campaign and analysis scripts, run-level results for all 31,680 runs, contrast tables and figures are archived in a public repository with a persistent identifier \cite{dataset}; \url{https://github.com/alanvallavaraj/liftsim}.
